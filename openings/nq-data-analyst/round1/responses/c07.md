## What I think you're actually asking for

You're asking whether a daily-timeframe NQ strategy can be made to survive a $2,000 trailing drawdown on one full-size contract. My read is that you already suspect the answer is no and you want to see whether I find out by arithmetic in an hour or by backtest in three weeks.

Here is the arithmetic, before any research. NQ is $20/point, tick 0.25 = $5.00. A $2,000 drawdown budget on one contract is **100 points of risk for the entire life of the account** — not per trade, total, and under a trailing threshold you never win that room back. NQ daily true range in the current regime runs roughly 1.5–1.9% of the index; at a 24–25k handle that is **~380–470 points**. I will recompute it from data rather than quote it, but the ratio is not close:

| | |
|---|---|
| Max points of risk (1 NQ, $2,000) | **100 pts** |
| NQ daily ATR(14), current regime | **~420 pts** |
| Ratio | **0.24× one day's range** |
| Stop at 0.5× ATR (~210 pts) | **$4,200 — 2.1× the whole budget** |

So the entire account is a quarter of one average day. That forces a dichotomy that no signal escapes:

- **Structurally-sized stop** (anything that respects daily-bar volatility, ≥150 pts): one trade can end the account. Untradeable.
- **Budget-sized stop** (20–30 pts, so you survive more than three trades): that is ~0.05–0.07× ATR. It is inside the bid-ask noise of a single 5-minute window. You are not trading a daily signal; you are buying a lottery ticket on whether NQ wiggles your way before it wiggles the other way, and you pay commission each time.

There is no parameter setting between those two. That is why I think the mandate as written is arithmetically unsound, and I'd rather tell you that today than in October.

**Four things the brief leaves implicit, each of which can independently kill it:**

1. **"Daily timeframe" implies holding overnight. Most $50k evals don't allow it.** Topstep-style rules require flat by 15:10 ET and no positions through the close; others permit overnight but cut the drawdown or the contract limit. If overnight holding is barred, "daily" can only mean *signals computed on daily bars, executed and closed intraday* — a completely different, and much harder, project. I need the rulebook before I write a line of code.
2. **Trailing on what?** EOD-closed-balance trailing and intraday-unrealized trailing are different games. Under intraday trailing, an open trade that goes +60 then closes +20 ratchets the floor up on the +60 and you are charged for the giveback. That makes **maximum adverse excursion, not trade PnL, the binding metric** — and daily-timeframe trades have enormous MAE. Also: does the threshold lock at the starting balance once you're up, and at what profit level? That single number changes the survival probability by an order of magnitude.
3. **Costs are not a rounding error here.** NQ round turn on a prop platform is ~$4.30 commission, plus my floor assumption of one tick of slippage per side ($10 RT) — call it **$14.30, or 0.72 NQ points, per round trip.** At one trade per session, 250 sessions, that's ~$3,575/yr: **1.8× the entire drawdown budget spent on friction alone.** Even at 60 trades a year it's 43% of the budget. Any daily strategy has to clear roughly 3 ticks per trade net before it has done anything.
4. **The quarterly roll gap is bigger than the account.** With front-end rates well above the NDX dividend yield, the NQ calendar spread is on the order of 0.7–0.9% of index per quarter — **~150–250 points, i.e. $3,000–$5,000 per roll, 1.5–2.5× the drawdown budget.** Four of those a year. If anyone hands me a pre-stitched continuous file and I don't check how it was adjusted, the backtest's entire PnL can be four phantom gaps. As it happens, **today is the third Friday of September — NQU6 settles on this morning's SOQ and the roll to NQZ6 went through around Sept 10.** Pull a continuous file right now and you are pulling it straight across a live roll.

## How I'd approach it

**Phase 0 — Constraints and arithmetic (half a day, before any data).**
Get the firm's actual rulebook PDF. Extract eight numbers: trail basis (EOD vs intraday), trail lock threshold, profit target, minimum trading days, max contracts, overnight/weekend policy, consistency rule (e.g. no day >30–50% of total profit — this alone forbids the lottery-ticket approach), and news-event restrictions. Then do the table above with the firm's real numbers and send you a one-page verdict. If the ratio stays where I expect, this phase is the deliverable and the rest is contingent on your call.

**Phase 1 — Empirical confirmation of the arithmetic (one day).**
Not a strategy. Three distributions, measured on real NQ:
- Daily true range by year, 2015–2026, and the share of sessions where TR > 100 points (I expect >95%).
- Overnight gap distribution across the 17:00–18:00 ET maintenance break and the Sunday 18:00 open, tail included — because a stop does not protect you through a gap.
- MAE from entry for a deliberately naive daily signal (e.g. long on close > 20-day MA), measured as: what fraction of eventually-profitable trades first traded 100 points against you? If that fraction is high — I expect 50–70% — then the $2,000 floor kills winning trades, and no amount of signal quality fixes it. **This is the single most decision-relevant number in the project** and it costs one day.

**Phase 2 — Data acquisition and fingerprinting.** Only if Phase 0/1 says proceed.
Databento GLBX.MDP3, **per-contract** NQ H/M/U/Z, OHLCV-1m plus definitions, 2015→present; CME DataMine for daily settlements, volume and open interest. Never a vendor continuous file as the sole series. Row count, first/last timestamp and a SHA per contract month, recorded.

**Phase 3 — Audit before analysis.** Duplicate and non-monotonic timestamps; zero-volume and zero-range bars; crossed/locked quotes; prints outside a rolling k-sigma band; the 17:00–18:00 ET break present every weekday; the Sunday 18:00 open; both US DST transitions and the ~3-week March and ~1-week Oct/Nov windows where US and EU DST are out of step; holidays and early closes reconciled against `pandas_market_calendars` CME Globex. Everything UTC in storage, reasoned in `America/Chicago`, displayed in `America/New_York`. Every exclusion quantified by count and date — nothing silently dropped.

**Phase 3a — The daily-bar definition question, which matters more than it sounds.** "Daily" on NQ is ambiguous three ways: the Globex session bar (18:00 ET prior day → 17:00 ET), the RTH bar (09:30–16:00 ET), and settle-vs-close (NQ settles on the 15:00 CT VWAP window, which is not the 16:00 ET print). A daily MA crossover gives materially different signals under each. I'd build all three and state which one the result depends on.

**Phase 4 — Continuous series, three ways.** Unadjusted per-contract, back-adjusted, ratio-adjusted. Roll dates from **observed volume/OI crossover**, verified quarter by quarter against the conventional Thursday eight days before third-Friday expiry, not assumed from it. Every roll gap logged with its size. Back-adjusted NQ over 10 years will push early history toward and possibly through zero — so percentage returns are distorted near the adjusted origin and I check for sign flips explicitly.

**Phase 5 — Run the strategy on all three series.** If results differ materially across adjustment methods, **that divergence is the headline finding**, and I localise it to specific roll dates before writing anything else.

**Phase 6 — Honest simulation.** The trailing floor is path-dependent, so this is an event loop, not vectorbt. Vectorbt is for screening the parameter surface; the reported number comes from bar-by-bar simulation of the floor under both EOD and intraday rules, with commission, one tick slippage floor, no fills outside the session filter, and the lock threshold modelled.

**Phase 7 — Survival, not average outcome.** Purged and embargoed walk-forward; stationary block bootstrap on returns; Monte Carlo permutation of trade order to get the distribution of worst paths and **P(touch the floor before the profit target)** under both trail rules. I report the 5th-percentile equity path, not the mean.

**Phase 8 — Try to kill it.** Parameters ±20%, entry shifted one bar, sample halved by era, one extra tick of slippage, breakeven slippage stated in ticks. Trial count declared and the Sharpe deflated for it.

Order matters because Phases 0 and 1 can end the project for $0 of data spend, and Phase 4 determines whether any number from Phase 5 means anything.

## Where this goes wrong

**1. Roll-gap phantom PnL.** A strategy shows a clean equity curve that is actually four 200-point unadjusted jumps a year. *Mitigation:* all three adjustment methods, gap sizes logged, and any surprising PnL reproduced against the unadjusted per-contract series before I attribute a single dollar to signal.

**2. The backtest models a fixed $2,000 stop instead of a trailing floor.** Enormously optimistic — a static floor lets you recover, a trailing one never does. *Mitigation:* the floor is simulated against the running high-water mark, under both EOD and intraday rules, and P(touch) is a reported metric, not a footnote.

**3. Intraday-trail MAE blindness.** Under an intraday trail, trades that close green can still take the account out via unrealised excursion. *Mitigation:* MAE distribution reported alongside PnL; if the trail is intraday, MAE is the primary metric and trade PnL is secondary.

**4. Fitting on a spike.** A daily-timeframe strategy over 10 years yields maybe 150–400 trades. With 250 trades and a measured Sharpe of 1.0, the 95% interval from the block bootstrap is roughly **[0.2, 1.8]** *before* deflating for trials — and after 200 sweep cells you need a raw Sharpe near 2.0 to clear the deflated threshold. *Mitigation:* trial count declared with every statistic; I report the parameter surface and the median of the plateau, never the best cell; if performance lives on a spike rather than a plateau I call it fitted and say so.

**5. Regime laundering.** 2015–2026 NQ is mostly a bull tape with two vol spikes. A long-biased daily rule will look brilliant and is measuring beta, not edge. *Mitigation:* era-split results reported separately, and long-only results shown against buy-and-hold on the same series with the same costs.

**6. Cost optimism.** Fills assumed at the close, no slippage, commission ignored. *Mitigation:* one tick per side as the floor for NQ (more at 09:30 and on stop orders), and **breakeven slippage in ticks reported for every result** — if the edge dies at 1.5 ticks it isn't an edge.

**7. Rule violation that isn't in the backtest at all.** The consistency rule (no single day exceeding ~30–50% of total profit) invalidates a passing curve that came from two big days. Same for news restrictions and minimum trading days. *Mitigation:* those rules go in the simulator as hard constraints, not as commentary.

**8. My own failure mode.** Delivering a pristine audited dataset and a pile of diagnostics that imply a conclusion I never actually stated. *Mitigation:* every deliverable leads with a verdict paragraph and a stated confidence, even when the confidence is low.
