## What I think you're actually asking for

You're asking whether a daily-timeframe NQ strategy can be made to survive a $2,000 trailing barrier on a $50,000 evaluation — and I think the honest answer is available before any signal research happens, from arithmetic alone.

The first number: `max_points_of_risk = 2000 / (20 × 1) = 100 points`. NQ NY-session ATR(14) has recently run roughly 410–450 points (~1.5–2% of index level; I'd re-measure on day one). So the entire lifetime buffer of the account is about **0.23 of one day's range**. That is not a stop — it's the total budget for every loss you will ever take, and on a trailing rule, profit moves the wall up behind you rather than building a cushion. To survive a three-loss sequence you need per-trade risk around 25–30 points, i.e. **~0.07 ATR**, which is inside the intraday noise of a single 5-minute bar. A daily-timeframe stop cannot live there.

Three things the brief leaves dangerously implicit:

1. **Whether the trail is end-of-day closing balance or intraday peak unrealised equity, and where it locks.** A $50k/$2,000 pairing reads like a Topstep-style Combine (EOD trail, locks at start + $100), but Apex-style accounts trail intraday and don't lock the same way. These are different problems, not different parameters. Under an intraday trail, an open position that goes +80 and comes back to flat has permanently spent 80 points of an 100-point budget while making nothing.
2. **Whether the account permits holding overnight at all.** Many evaluation programs require flat before the 17:00 ET close, or restrict holds around scheduled releases. If overnight holds are barred, "daily timeframe" is impossible *by rule*, not by arithmetic, and the mandate self-terminates.
3. **Overnight gap risk.** A daily-hold NQ position is exposed to the session break. NQ close-to-open gaps routinely exceed 100 points. The account can be ended by a gap with no adverse intraday move at all — the stop is not a stop across the break.

There's also a horizon problem nobody has stated: an evaluation runs weeks, and a daily strategy produces maybe 10–25 trades in that window. Pass/fail is then dominated by sequence variance, not by edge. This is a **first-passage survival problem under a path-dependent barrier**, not a strategy-selection problem, and it should be modelled that way from the start.

## How I'd approach it

**Phase 0 — mandate arithmetic and rulebook (day one, ~2 hours).** The range-to-budget check above, plus the actual prop firm rule PDF. Output is a go/no-go on the mandate as written, with the four options priced: switch to MNQ, shorten the holding period, widen the budget, or size it explicitly as a lottery ticket. If MNQ is permitted, `1000 / (2 × 1) = 1,000 points` of risk — 2.3 ATR — and a genuine daily strategy with a 150–250 point stop and four to six losses of buffer becomes arithmetically possible. That single substitution is the highest-value decision in the project and it costs nothing to evaluate.

**Phase 1 — data and provenance.** Databento `GLBX.MDP3` for NQ daily and 1-minute (I need intraday bars to compute true MAE, because daily-bar backtests silently assume the stop wasn't touched intraday), Norgate or CSI for long back-adjusted daily history covering multiple regimes, CBOE for VXN/VIX9D/VIX3M/VVIX and VX term structure, CME CVOL for NQ implied vol. Roll rule pre-registered: volume/open-interest crossover on the Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec cycle, typically three to five sessions before the third Friday. I carry two series — back-adjusted for signal generation, unadjusted per-contract for PnL and stop placement — and I never let a roll gap become PnL. One specific trap: NQ was in the low thousands a decade ago and is in five figures now, so **every threshold has to be expressed in ATR units or percent, never in points**, or the backtest is comparing incommensurable things across its own history.

**Phase 2 — pre-registration, before results.** A dated file containing: VXN buckets with breakpoints fixed from a burn-in window (not full-sample quantiles); VX term-structure state from front/second-month and VIX9D/VIX3M; trend state from 200-day slope and price-versus-200d; dispersion from NDX-versus-equal-weight or implied correlation; calendar states for peak earnings weeks (late Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct, which expand daily ATR 20–35%), FOMC, monthly OPEX, quarter-end. Success criteria and the evidence bar go in the same file: **any cell with fewer than five independent episodes spanning two calendar years is reported INCONCLUSIVE**, positive or negative.

**Phase 3 — test the series before the strategy.** ADF and KPSS together on target and on the signal's own distribution, `ruptures`/Bai–Perron for structural breaks, CUSUM for parameter drift. If the signal's distribution has broken, that's the finding and I report it before any PnL.

**Phase 4 — backtest once, costs in from the start.** Actual contract, 0.25 tick, one tick per side RTH and two overnight or in the first minute after 09:30, plus $4 round-turn commission — labelled assumptions, and the headline re-run at double.

**Phase 5 — stratify, then aggregate.** Conditional expectancy, hit rate, MAE/MFE and Sharpe per regime cell with episode counts, before any full-sample line appears. If more than ~70% of cumulative PnL comes from one vol bucket or one term-structure state, that is the headline: it's a regime bet, and I'll say so in those words.

**Phase 6 — attack it.** Deflated Sharpe with the true trial count, PBO via CSCV, stationary block bootstrap with block length matched to autocorrelation, Newey–West, SPA if more than one variant was screened.

**Phase 7 — survival.** Monte Carlo over trade-sequence orderings under both EOD and intraday trailing, reporting probability of breaching the floor and the 5th-percentile path — not a single historical max drawdown dressed up as a risk estimate.

**Phase 8 — is it short vol in disguise?** If conditional performance tracks the VX curve state or NDX 25-delta risk reversal, the edge is a variance risk premium harvest and should be priced as such, not sold as alpha.

2. Pull ten years of NQ daily and measure the **close-to-open gap distribution** — specifically the fraction of sessions where the overnight gap alone exceeds 100 points, and the same conditioned on VXN bucket. My prior is that this alone kills the NQ version.

5. Send you the kill memo with the four options and a recommendation. The full study gets a stated start date after you choose.

## Where this goes wrong

- **Trail ambiguity.** Modelling EOD when the firm trails intraday produces a confidently wrong breach probability. *Mitigation:* every survival number reported twice, both variants, until the rulebook is confirmed.
- **Daily bars hide the intraday path.** A daily-bar backtest that never touches the stop is fiction. *Mitigation:* MAE computed from 1-minute bars; stop-hit determination on intraday data only.
- **Roll contamination.** Back-adjusted series produce phantom gains at the roll and mis-stated stop distances. *Mitigation:* dual series, PnL only from unadjusted per-contract.
- **Point-denominated thresholds across a 10× index move.** *Mitigation:* everything in ATR or percent units.
- **A 2022-downtrend bet wearing a costume.** The single most likely outcome of this project is a strategy carried by two or three clustered episodes. *Mitigation:* conditional table before aggregate, 70% concentration rule, episode counts in every cell.
- **Sweeping and reporting the best cell.** *Mitigation:* full surface, trial count, deflated Sharpe; if the surface is a spike rather than a plateau I call it curve-fitting by name.
- **My own framing becoming unfalsifiable.** "A new regime" explains every live drawdown. *Mitigation:* regime-attribution debt — if I attribute a live loss to a regime, in the same report I state the forward-observable condition and the date or trade count by which performance should resume. If it doesn't, my story was wrong in writing and the strategy retires rather than gets re-explained.
- **Passing the evaluation and calling it validation.** With 15 trades, a coin can pass. *Mitigation:* the pass/fail probability is pre-computed; a pass inside the pre-computed lucky band is reported as luck.
- **Execution realism I don't have.** If the edge depends on fills at the open, at settlement, or around releases, or is smaller than four ticks per round turn, I flag it for whoever owns execution rather than guessing at queue position.

Concretely: no more than 70% of PnL from one vol bucket; at least five independent episodes per live cell across two calendar years; Monte Carlo breach probability under 25% on the intraday-trail assumption; and the edge surviving at double the cost assumption.
