## What I think you're actually asking for

You're asking whether a $2,000 drawdown budget can carry a one-lot NQ position held on a daily horizon. It can't, and I think the brief already half-knows that — otherwise you wouldn't have specified "not the micro" and "not a scalper" so pointedly. Those two constraints are what make it fail, and they're the two you'd have to give up.

The arithmetic, before any signal:

```
max_points_of_risk = drawdown_budget / (point_value × contracts)
                   = $2,000 / ($20 × 1)
                   = 100 NQ points — for the entire evaluation, not per trade
```

100 points is the whole account. Against a daily ATR(14) on NQ that has run roughly 250–450 points in recent regimes (**I do not know today's value — that's a day-one measurement, not a memory**), the entire drawdown budget is something like **0.25–0.4× one day's ordinary range**. My escalation threshold is an implied stop under one third of ATR, and this is the whole budget, not the stop. If you want even five losing trades of room before failure, per-trade risk is 20 points — about 5% of ATR. On a daily bar that isn't a stop, it's noise. You'd be flat before 09:45 most sessions.

Three things the brief leaves dangerously implicit:

1. **"Daily timeframe" means holding through the Globex session.** A stop resting at 02:00 ET gets filled where liquidity is, not where you put it. NQ top-of-book is a handful of lots even at midday; overnight it's thinner. A 100-point total budget cannot survive one bad overnight print, and you don't need a macro event — the Nasdaq-100 is ~45% seven names, so a daily NQ position is substantially an NVDA/MSFT/AAPL earnings-calendar position. Those dates are known in advance and are not a tail risk, they're a scheduled one.
2. **"Maximum drawdown" is underspecified and it's the single most important term.** Trailing on closing balance, or intraday on unrealized peak? Does it stop trailing once you're above start+$2,000, or ratchet forever? On an intraday-unrealized trail, a winning trade that runs +150 then gives back 80 has spent 80% of your budget while making money. Same strategy, two rules, completely different survival probability. I won't pick between them — that's a CEO decision and I'd escalate it.
3. **The eval has a profit target too.** Typically ~$3,000 on a $50k. That's 150 points of profit against 100 points of room — you must make one and a half times your entire risk budget before you lose it. That's a first-passage problem with absorbing barriers, and it's computable on day one before I touch a single price series.

Also: "passing the evaluation" and "having an edge" are different objectives. A 100-point budget can be passed by one lucky trade. I'd want to know which you're buying.

## How I'd approach it

**Phase 0 — mandate arithmetic and rulebook (day 1).** The numbers above, plus the actual firm rulebook. This phase can kill the project, which is why it's first and why it's cheap.

**Phase 1 — feasibility Monte Carlo, no data, no signal (day 1–2).** Simulate the barrier problem: +$3,000 target, -$2,000 trailing floor, under both EOD-close and intraday-unrealized trail rules, across a grid of (per-trade edge, stop size, contracts). Include the zero-edge baseline. This tells you what per-trade edge the mandate *requires*. If the required edge is implausible for a daily NQ signal, we stop and I write that up. I'm not going to quote you a pass probability here because I haven't run it.

**Phase 2 — data, before signal.** Databento GLBX.MDP3 for NQ, ~8 years to span 2018 vol, 2020, the 2022 bear, and the 2023–25 trend. Everything in ET, DST-aware. I keep **per-contract unadjusted alongside a back-adjusted continuous series** and name the method in the report, because any level-based signal (prior-day high, moving average, breakout) is an artefact of the adjustment choice. I plot front- and next-month volume through every roll and confirm the roll date matches where liquidity actually moved — for NQ that's around the Thursday eight days before the third Friday, but I verify it rather than assume it. Cash settlement to the third-Friday SOQ means there's no delivery cliff, but there is a liquidity cliff. And I define "daily bar" explicitly: CME session (18:00–17:00 ET with the 17:00–18:00 break) or RTH (09:30–16:00). They give different OHLC and different signals; picking silently is a way to get a free parameter.

**Phase 3 — fill model before signal.** Spread by hour, no touches-equals-fill on limits without a queue assumption, no stop filled at the stop price. Specifically for this mandate: a **gap-through model** — stop fills at the next available print with a depth-based sweep, not at the trigger. Commissions and exchange fees as an explicit per-round-turn dollar figure with the date I verified it. At one lot and daily frequency, costs are ~$5 RT and maybe $100/month — they are *not* the binding constraint here and I'd say so rather than dressing them up as analysis. The binding constraint is risk geometry. `pytest` on the fill model and the roll logic specifically.

**Phase 4 — pre-registration.** One sentence of mechanism (who is on the other side and why they're slow), the parameter grid, the pass criteria. Committed, hash recorded. Every variant tested after that gets counted.

**Phase 5 — in-sample only, walk-forward with purged and embargoed gaps.** Never a single split. Holdout — last 18 months — locked from day one, separate directory, not reachable by the engine.

**Phase 6 — count the searching.** Deflated Sharpe, PBO via CSCV, White's Reality Check if I'm comparing a family of rules. An isolated spike in the parameter surface gets reported as noise, not as a result. If I've run more than thirty configurations I want a second pair of eyes on the multiple-testing treatment — standing requirement, not a courtesy.

**Phase 7 — survival, not average outcome.** Monte Carlo over trade orderings plus stationary block bootstrap; report the distribution of max drawdown and the percentage of paths breaching the floor under *both* trail rules.

**Phase 8 — stress.** Excluding roll weeks, excluding the first and last fifteen minutes of RTH, excluding FOMC/CPI, excluding the three trading days around the top-7 constituents' earnings, and with slippage doubled. Each as a line item. If the edge only lives in one of those windows, that's a finding, and I say it rather than averaging it away.

**Phase 9 — holdout once.** Whatever it says, it wins.

The order matters because Phases 0–1 cost a day and can return a verdict. Doing them after a month of backtesting is how you end up defending a strategy instead of evaluating one.

## What I'd do in the first day

1. **Read the firm's actual rulebook PDF**, not the marketing page. I need: trailing basis (EOD close vs intraday unrealized), whether the trail locks at start+$2,000 or ratchets forever, profit target, minimum trading days, consistency rule, whether overnight and weekend holds are permitted at all, and the flat-by time if there is one. Many evaluation accounts either prohibit holding through the daily maintenance window or charge full exchange overnight margin — if overnight holds are barred, "daily timeframe on NQ" is over in the first hour for a reason that has nothing to do with statistics.
2. **Verify NQ margin and fees against the current CME spec sheet**, dated in the writeup. I hold the contract mechanics without lookup — $20/point, 0.25 tick = $5.00, quarterly H/M/U/Z, cash-settled to the third-Friday SOQ, settlement off 15:59:30–16:00:00 — but margin and fees move and I don't cite them from memory.
3. **Pull NQ daily and 1-minute from Databento** and print three numbers: current ATR(14); the distribution of 16:00→09:30 moves; and where 100 points sits as a percentile of each. My prior — labelled **HYPOTHESIS (UNTESTED)** — is that a 100-point overnight move is a routine occurrence rather than a tail, but that's exactly the kind of remembered impression I don't let into a conclusion until it has a count against the full sample.
4. **Write the barrier Monte Carlo** from Phase 1 and run the zero-edge case.
5. **Write and commit the pre-registration file** with the hash, even though I expect to report NOT TRADEABLE — because if I'm wrong about that I'll want the file to already exist.

By end of day one I can tell you whether this is a research project or a two-page memo.

## Where this goes wrong

- **The trailing rule is misread.** Highest-probability failure. A strategy that survives comfortably under EOD trailing can fail 60% of paths under intraday-unrealized trailing, and nobody notices until the account is dead. *Fix:* simulate both, report both, escalate the difference to you rather than picking.
- **Stops assumed to fill at the stop price.** On a daily-horizon NQ position this is not a rounding error, it's the whole result. A 100-point budget is one gap. *Fix:* gap-through fill model, depth-based sweep, and every result stated as conditional on that model with an explicit statement of what changes if the model is wrong.
- **Back-adjusted series faking level-based signals.** NQ back-adjusted over eight years shifts by hundreds of points; a "breakout of the 60-day high" on that series is partly an artefact of roll accounting. *Fix:* per-contract unadjusted alongside, roll modelled as an actual trade paying the calendar spread, method named in the report.
- **Regime survivorship.** 2023–2025 NQ trended hard. Any long-biased daily filter looks brilliant on that sample. *Fix:* sample spans multiple vol regimes and I report per-regime, not pooled.
- **Statistical power.** This is the second fatal problem after risk geometry. A daily signal over eight years might produce 100–200 trades. With that count you cannot separate a true Sharpe of 0.8 from zero — you'd need something closer to a decade to detect it at reasonable power. Any daily strategy I hand you with 80 trades and a great Sharpe is overfit, and deflated Sharpe will say so. *Fix:* pre-registered grid, hard stop when exhausted, DSR and PBO reported, and I refuse to keep searching after the grid is done. If nothing passes, the answer is that nothing passed.
- **Passing the eval mistaken for having an edge.** A tiny budget rewards lottery tickets. *Fix:* report P(pass) and edge quality as separate numbers, and flag loudly if the single best day is more than ~20% of total PnL.
- **Me.** I'm prone to treating one vivid session as evidence and to tinkering past the point where the grid said stop. *Fix:* every anecdote written as `HYPOTHESIS (UNTESTED)` and converted into a query with a count; variant counter; holdout locked from day one because I know I'd peek; nothing leaves a notebook — results go to parquet with a JSON run manifest through the versioned engine or they don't get reported. When my read of the tape disagrees with the deflated Sharpe, the DSR wins and I write down that I disagreed.
- **Someone proposes averaging down** to rescue the budget. I won't model martingale or grid-into-loss on a trailing account. The floor never falls back, so there is no recovery path — it converts a losing trade into account death with extra steps.

## What I'd need from you

**Decisions I need and won't make alone:**

- **Which prop firm, and the rulebook.** If I don't get it, I assume the most restrictive reading — intraday trailing on unrealized peak, ratcheting, no lock, $3,000 target, 10 minimum days, no overnight hold — and I state that assumption in the first line of the report.
- **Is MNQ genuinely off the table?** MNQ at $2/point gives 1,000 points of budget instead of 100 — 10× finer granularity, which is the one change that makes a daily-horizon structural stop fit. The brief says NQ, so I won't substitute silently. Absent an answer I report NOT TRADEABLE on NQ and present MNQ as option one.
- **Which are you buying: a passed evaluation, or a strategy with a demonstrable edge?** Absent an answer I assume the edge, and report eval feasibility separately.
- **Are you willing to change the holding period?** Shortening to intraday makes the arithmetic work but is a different strategy from the one you asked for, and I'd want you to say that out loud rather than have me quietly deliver it.
- **Will you accept "NOT TRADEABLE" as a completed deliverable?** I'd rather know now. A failed strategy with a specific reason is a finished piece of work in my view; if it isn't in yours, that's a real difference worth surfacing in a first-round interview.

**Access and resources:** Databento budget (for a daily horizon, 1-minute OHLCV plus top-of-book is sufficient — I'd only go to MBO if queue position around stop fills turns out to matter, and I'd ask before spending it); CME DataMine for official settlements, volume and open interest to locate rolls; ideally one broker or Sierra Chart tick export of a session I can reconcile tick-by-tick against a source I traded through, before I trust the vendor data. If I can't reconcile a dataset against a second source, that's an escalation, not a footnote. And a second analyst's hour on the multiple-testing treatment if the search exceeds thirty configurations.

**One thing outside the brief:** if what you actually want is daily-horizon Nasdaq exposure with a genuinely hard $2,000 loss cap, a defined-risk options structure on NQ gives you a real floor with no gap risk, which a futures stop does not. Prop evaluation accounts almost universally forbid options, so I expect the answer is no — but it's the honest answer to the underlying problem and I'd rather say it than pretend the futures version is the only shape available. Worth one question to you.

## How you'll know it worked

**Within week one**, you should have a one-page memo with the points-of-risk-versus-ATR arithmetic shown, the zero-edge pass probability under both trail rules, and a verdict. My expectation, stated now so you can hold me to it: **NOT TRADEABLE as written**, with four options — move to MNQ, shorten the holding period (and accept that's a different strategy), raise the drawdown budget to roughly the $25–35k range that a one-lot daily NQ structural stop actually needs, or take it as a stated-low-probability lottery ticket. If that memo arrives in week one rather than month three, the process worked regardless of the answer.

**If it survives to a full research cycle**, success looks like: holdout result agreeing with in-sample inside the block-bootstrap CI; deflated Sharpe positive after counting every configuration including the ones added post-registration; PBO below ~0.3; the parameter surface a plateau rather than a spike; worst-path drawdown from Monte Carlo inside budget on a stated majority of paths under the *intraday* trail, not just EOD; and a stated minimum viable account size in contracts.

**Signs it failed even if the equity curve looks good:** an equity curve shown without trade count, net-of-fee expectancy in ticks and dollars, deflated Sharpe and worst-path drawdown printed beside it; edge concentrated in fewer than ~30 trades or one calendar quarter; a single day contributing more than ~20% of PnL; a result that only exists on the back-adjusted series; or me coming back with "I found something" after the pre-registered grid was exhausted. Any of those, you should push back hard.
