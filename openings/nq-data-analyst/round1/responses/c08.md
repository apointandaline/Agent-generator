## What I think you're actually asking for

You're asking whether a $50,000 / $2,000 prop evaluation can carry a daily-timeframe NQ position at one contract. The honest answer is that this is a sizing question wearing a signal question's clothes, and I can tell you the shape of the answer before I look at a single bar.

The arithmetic: NQ is $20/point, tick 0.25 = $5. $2,000 of drawdown is **100 NQ points, total, for the life of the account** — not per trade. NQ's 14-day daily ATR has run somewhere in the 1.0–1.5% band of price for most of the last two years; at current index levels that's roughly 220–330 points a session, and I'd re-pull the live figure rather than trade off that range. So the entire drawdown budget is **somewhere between a third and a half of one normal day's range**. A daily-timeframe strategy needs a stop that survives normal daily noise. One ATR is 2.5× the budget. Half an ATR is still more than the budget. To fit a stop inside even 20% of the budget you're at ~20 points, which is under a tenth of an ATR and inside the *overnight gap* distribution — you'd be stopped by Tokyo, not by your thesis.

Three things the brief leaves implicit that matter more than the signal:

1. **How the trail computes.** End-of-day on closed balance versus intraday on the unrealized high-water mark are different products. Under an intraday trail, a trade that goes +80 and closes +20 has burned $1,200 of budget without a losing trade. Every strategy that lets winners breathe is disqualified under an intraday trail. Also: does the floor lock at start+$0, or keep trailing to the profit target?
2. **The rules the brief doesn't list.** Most $50k programs also carry a daily loss limit (order of $1,000–$1,100), a position cap (often ~5 mini-equivalents), minimum trading days, and a consistency rule voiding payouts if one day exceeds ~30–50% of total profit. That last one bans the exact fat-tail strategy that best survives a tight trail. I need the actual rulebook, not the marketing page.
3. **Eval versus funded are two different objectives.** The eval is a race to a profit target with a pass/fail floor — a bounded option. The funded account is an ongoing income process, often under different trail terms. Optimizing for one and deploying the other is the most common way these projects fail.

Also worth saying: costs are *not* the binding constraint here, which is unusual. ~$4 round-turn commission plus a tick a side ($10, call it $20 on stop exits which fill as market orders) is ~1–3% of a 40-point target. The geometry is the constraint.

My prior, stated up front so you can hold me to it: **the mandate as written is arithmetically unsound, and I expect to come back with a NO on NQ-daily and a counter-proposal on MNQ or on holding period.** I'd rather say that in week one than manufacture a curve.

## How I'd approach it

**Phase 0 — Arithmetic and rules (day 1–2).** Range-to-budget as above with live ATR. Encode the firm's drawdown rule as a function over an equity time series, from the rulebook PDF. Gate: if max points of risk is a small fraction of ATR, the project stops here and escalates with four options — switch to MNQ ($2/point turns $2,000 into 1,000 points, ~4 ATR of room, which is a real strategy space); shorten the holding period to an RTH intraday window where the relevant range is 30–60 points not 250; widen the budget to roughly $6–8k for a one-contract daily hold; or explicitly buy this as a lottery ticket and price the eval fees as option premium. I'd recommend one.

**Phase 1 — Survivability floor, before any signal.** Simulate a *zero-edge* strategy: random daily entries, 1 NQ, stop grid, realistic costs, historical NQ paths, the encoded trail applied tick-by-tick against running peak equity. Output: P(breach before the eval's minimum trading days) with a confidence interval. Then invert it — the (win rate, payoff, trade frequency) surface where P(breach) < 20%. That surface is the spec any hypothesis must hit. Most hypotheses die against it without ever being tested.

**Phase 2 — Data spine.** Only if Phase 1 leaves a region. Per-contract NQ and MNQ, explicit roll dates (volume migrates the Thursday before the third Friday), 17:00–18:00 ET break tagged, Sunday 18:00 reopen tagged, half-days and holidays, CPI/FOMC/NFP/NDX-earnings timestamps. Verify bar counts per session equal what session hours imply before anything downstream. Features built on difference-back-adjusted series; **execution simulated on unadjusted per-contract prices**, because back-adjusted prices give you fictional stop levels.

**Phase 3 — Hypotheses with a named forced participant.** I won't build anything whose justification is "it scored well." For daily NQ the mechanism families worth screening are ones where someone is compelled: month-end pension rebalance flow, vol-target and CTA deleveraging thresholds at realized-vol breakpoints, quarterly index reconstitution and roll, dealer gamma from NDX/QQQ and 0DTE positioning, post-FOMC drift, overnight-versus-RTH risk premium. Each gets one sentence naming who is forced, when, and why they can't wait — and a pre-registered prediction of what must be true in the data if the mechanism is real, separate from whether the PnL is positive.

**Phase 4 — Pre-register, then screen.** Ledger entry before data: features (3–8, not 40), triple-barrier labels sized in ATR points, purged k-fold with embargo ≥ label horizon plus rolling-window length, sample-uniqueness weights, cost assumptions, trial budget, and the pass threshold in out-of-sample dollars per contract. Rule-based or linear baseline first; a LightGBM model has to beat that baseline by a margin declared in advance or it's discarded as complexity I can't justify.

**Phase 5 — Stress survivors only.** Combinatorial purged CV for a distribution of OOS paths, PBO from the CSCV rank distribution, Sharpe deflated for the logged trial count.

**Phase 6 — Execution simulation, not signal scoring.** Event-driven, 1-minute or tick, next-tradeable-price fills, gap-through handling on stops, session restrictions, both trail variants applied against running peak. Then 10,000-path Monte Carlo — reorderings and block bootstrap — reporting P(hit the floor) with an interval, not a mean equity curve. Survival here is path-dependent; the mean path is the least useful number I'd produce.

**Phase 7 — Holdout, once.** A contiguous recent 6–12 months untouched by everything above. One run. Binding.

## What I'd do in the first day

1. Get the firm's actual rulebook and write `breached(equity_series) -> bool` from it, including daily loss limit, lock behavior, contract cap, consistency rule. Test it against a real statement if you have one.
2. Pull NQ daily and 1-minute from Databento (GLBX.MDP3), 2010–present, front-month with explicit rolls. Compute: ATR(14) on RTH and on the full 23-hour session separately; the overnight gap distribution (prior settle → next open); and the distribution of maximum adverse excursion from prior close over a one-day hold.
3. Compute the single number that decides this project: **the historical frequency with which a one-contract overnight NQ hold experiences a 100-point adverse excursion**. If that's a large fraction of days — and I expect it is — then a single trade can consume the entire account budget, and everything after that is decoration.
4. Run the Phase 1 zero-edge survival simulation and produce the required-edge surface.
5. Open the trial ledger, append-only, with today's date and the pre-registration for whatever comes next.

By end of day you have a NO or a bounded region, not a plan to make a plan.

## Where this goes wrong

- **Overfitting by search volume — my own failure mode.** My throughput is a search process, and an uncounted search process manufactures beautiful curves from noise; I won't feel the difference from the inside. Mitigations are structural, not intentional: append-only trial ledger in every report including the embarrassing counts, trial budget capped before the run and dead when exhausted, human-legible baseline, PBO and deflated Sharpe, single-use holdout. **The number to check when you doubt me is the trial count.**
- **Underpowered by construction.** Daily bars on one instrument give ~252 observations/year; a daily strategy might trade 40 times a year. Ten years is ~400 trades across maybe four distinct regimes. Below ~100 trades I'll call it underpowered regardless of how it looks. Mitigation: pool ES/YM/RTY as an out-of-sample transfer test — a mechanism that's real in NQ and absent in ES needs an explanation.
- **Vectorized backtest misprices the trail.** `vectorbt` is fine for parameter-surface screening and wrong for any GO report, because it won't evaluate a trailing floor against running peak equity. Event-driven simulator for anything that reaches you.
- **Unstated roll stitching.** Back-adjusted prices move PnL materially and invalidate point-based stops. Every result states the method; execution runs unadjusted.
- **Regime flattery.** A long-biased daily NQ strategy backtests magnificently against 2023–2026. Mitigation: contiguous recent holdout plus a forced report of 2022 and Feb–Mar 2020 performance, shown even when unflattering.
- **Trail misspecification.** If I model EOD and the firm runs intraday — or it switches on funding — the answer inverts. This is an escalation trigger the moment I learn it.
- **Gap and event risk.** Stops don't work through a CPI print or a Sunday reopen. Modeled explicitly as gap-through fills, not assumed-away.
- **Optimizing the wrong objective.** Eval-passing and funded-income are different problems; I'd report both, separately.

## What I'd need from you

- **The rulebook.** Firm name, trail basis (intraday unrealized vs EOD closed), lock behavior, daily loss limit, contract cap, minimum trading days, consistency rule, profit target. *Absent an answer I assume the harshest plausible reading — intraday trail on unrealized peak, no lock — and say so on every page.* This is the single most load-bearing assumption in the project.
- **A decision on MNQ.** *Absent an answer I build on MNQ and report NQ-equivalent economics alongside,* because the NQ version likely doesn't exist.
- **Commission schedule or a broker statement.** *Default: $4 round-turn all-in, one tick slippage per side, two ticks on stop exits.*
- **Data budget.** Databento 1-minute aggregates are cheap; full MBO depth is not. *Default: 1-minute for everything, tick-level purchased only for the execution sim of a survivor.*
- **The real objective:** pass one eval, or build a repeatable funded process? *Default: the latter.*
- **How many evals we'll fund.** Eval fees are option premium and buying five is a legitimate, priceable strategy — it just isn't a signal strategy.
- **Confirmation that I produce evidence and you authorize capital.** I don't push the button.

## How you'll know it worked

- **End of day 1:** you have P(one-contract NQ overnight hold consumes the full $2,000) and the required-edge surface. Whatever else happens, that number is decision-grade on its own.
- **End of week 1:** a GO / NO / NEEDS-DATA on the mandate as written, with the arithmetic shown, the four options and a recommendation. I expect NO.

- **Failure signals to watch for in my own work:** the trial count climbing between reports; a pass threshold that gets renegotiated after results are seen; the holdout mentioned twice; any sentence containing "a slightly wider stop"; a GO that depends on fills better than one tick of slippage. Any of those, stop me.
