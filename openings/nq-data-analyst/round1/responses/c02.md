## What I think you're actually asking for

You're asking whether a $2,000 trailing drawdown can carry one NQ contract on a daily holding period. It can't, and I'd rather say that in the first meeting than after three weeks of backtesting.

The arithmetic: NQ is $20/point, 0.25 tick, $5/tick. $2,000 ÷ $20 = **100 points of total room** — not per trade, the whole account. My remembered figure for NQ's 14-day ATR is roughly 400–500 points and I won't design off a remembered number, but even at 400 that makes your entire drawdown budget **about 0.25 ATR**. A quarter-ATR stop on one NQ is $2,000. There is no stop distance on this product that is simultaneously wide enough to survive noise and narrow enough to survive the account.

Three things the brief leaves implicit that determine the whole design:

1. **"Daily timeframe" is two different mandates.** Daily-bar *signals* and *overnight holds* are separable. NQ's positive drift is disproportionately a close-to-open phenomenon; intraday is closer to flat with mean-reverting character. So a daily strategy that flattens at the close forgoes most of the edge it's trying to capture, and one that holds overnight eats a gap distribution whose upper decile is a large multiple of $2,000. That tension *is* the project. Which one you mean changes everything.
2. **The drawdown rule is unspecified and it is the binding design input.** EOD trail against closing balance, or intraday trail against *peak unrealised* equity? Does it lock at initial balance + buffer once you're up? A strategy that passes under EOD trailing can be mathematically dead under intraday trailing, because intraday trailing prices your MFE against you. I will not guess this.
3. **The objective isn't Sharpe, it's a first-passage probability.** $50K/$2K with a profit target (probably $3,000), a daily loss cap (probably $1,000), and likely a consistency rule means the goal is *P(reach target before hitting the absorbing floor)*. A high-expectancy strategy with occasional 300-point adverse excursions has a pass probability near zero regardless of its hundred-trade expectancy. Optimising expectancy here optimises the wrong quantity.

My honest read: **as written, this does not hold up.** The four ways out, in the order I'd rank them — (a) trade MNQ at $2/pt, which buys 1,000 points ≈ 2.4 ATR of room and makes the problem real; (b) keep NQ but shorten the hold to intraday with flat-by-close, accepting that this is no longer the strategy you asked for; (c) raise the drawdown budget to ~$8–10K, which is what one NQ on daily holds actually needs; (d) run it as a stated lottery ticket with the ruin probability written down in advance. I recommend (a), and I'd note the cost objection is smaller than people assume: round-turn friction is ~0.7 NQ points versus ~1.1 MNQ points, which on a 200-point daily move is noise.

*One disclosure up front: my instincts were formed on a bank index desk where a hedge could be adjusted continuously and one contract was never the minimum unit of risk. I'll flag in writing anywhere that's leaking into a recommendation.*

## How I'd approach it

**Phase 0 — mechanics and range-to-budget (half a day).** Nail the product spec, the CME session calendar (18:00 ET Sunday open, the daily 17:00–18:00 ET break, quarterly Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec — verified against the current CME calendar, not memory), and the complete prop rule set. Then run the room ÷ ATR check above on live data. If it fails — and I expect it to — I stop here and bring you the four options. I don't parameter-search my way out of an arithmetic problem.

**Phase 1 — data, before any hypothesis.** Databento GLBX.MDP3 for NQ 1-minute OHLCV and MBP-1 around stop levels; CME DataMine for settlements. Build the continuous series with a declared roll — volume-crossover, typically the Thursday ~8 days before the third-Friday expiry. I'd run **signals on difference-adjusted back-adjusted** data (NQ strategies are additive in points) and **P&L on per-contract unadjusted** so roll slippage is real, and verify across at least two quarterly rolls that no phantom gap was manufactured. The roll method goes in every report; an unstated one makes the number meaningless.

**Phase 2 — measure the constraint before building anything.** Empirical distributions of close-to-open gap, session true range, and — the one that matters — **adverse excursion from entry within N sessions**. If the 60th percentile of one-session MAE exceeds 100 points, the mandate is dead on NQ and I have the number to prove it rather than an intuition.

**Phase 3 — hypothesis as mechanism, not pattern.** Written before the backtest: why the edge should exist, and what must be true in the data if it's real. Candidates I'd rank: overnight drift capture conditioned on realised-vol regime; ES/NQ relative-vol mean reversion; variance risk premium harvest expressed in futures sizing rather than in options.

**Phase 4 — surface layer, only if testable, and only the cheap version first.** VXN term-structure slope, NDX 25-delta risk reversal, and gamma-by-strike from free OCC/CME open interest. Every surface reading terminates in a futures instruction — *expect range compression, halve size; expect expansion, stand aside* — or it doesn't go in the report. The dealer-sign assumption is unverifiable from public data and gets labelled an assumption every single time. Unconditional results reported alongside conditional ones so the conditioning's marginal contribution is visible.

**Phase 5 — one backtest, costs in from the first run.** $4 round-turn commission plus one tick per side minimum, more at the open, on gaps, and into expiry.

**Phase 6 — attack it.** Walk-forward with genuinely held-back OOS, Politis–Romano stationary block bootstrap for the CI, deflated Sharpe against the number of configurations I actually tried, and Monte Carlo over *trade orderings* to get the distribution of max drawdown and P(breach). Score survival under **both** EOD and intraday trailing and report both numbers.

## Where this goes wrong

- **Overfitting on a tiny effective sample.** ~4,000 daily sessions since 2010, and overlapping/regime-clustered trades. Power analysis first: to reject at t=2 a strategy whose true Sharpe is 0.5 needs ~16 years — the entire history. So a daily-bar NQ strategy with sub-0.8 true Sharpe **cannot be validated to significance with available data**. Mitigation: state the required N up front, pre-register the rule, report deflated Sharpe and number of variants tried, and accept "underpowered" as a finding.
- **Scoring intraday trailing drawdown off daily bars.** You cannot — daily OHLC doesn't tell you the path, and peak-unrealised trailing is path-dependent. Mitigation: 1-minute minimum, MBP-1 where stops sit.
- **Regime non-stationarity.** 2010–2026 is not one population. 0DTE growth from 2022 changed the intraday/overnight return split and the mean-reversion character materially. Mitigation: test on the post-2022 subsample separately and report both; name the regimes the sample lacks.
- **Earnings-week ATR expansion.** Late Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct expand NQ range 20–35%. A backtest whose drawdowns cluster there will fail live in a different month. Mitigation: report drawdown by calendar week, and conditional sizing.
- **Look-ahead through settlement.** Using the settlement print to trade "at the close" is a classic. Mitigation: all fills at next-bar open with modelled slippage.
- **Passing the evaluation and dying funded.** Different rules, different sizing, different psychology. Mitigation: report pass rate under both rule sets and the post-funding 90-day survival probability separately.
- **Me drifting into surface work.** It's more interesting than it's useful to an NQ execution decision. Mitigation: the room ÷ ATR check runs before any vol analysis, and any options conclusion without a futures-side instruction gets deleted.

## How you'll know it worked

**Success is a defensible decision within three weeks, and "don't fund this on NQ" is a success.** Concretely:

- The room ÷ ATR arithmetic and the adverse-excursion distribution are on paper, and you can make the NQ-vs-MNQ-vs-budget call from them without taking my word for anything.
- Any strategy I bring you has: expectancy in points *and* ticks net of costs, trade count, bootstrap CI, deflated Sharpe with the variant count disclosed, roll method stated, and a Monte Carlo distribution of max drawdown with P(breach) under **both** trailing rules.
- The out-of-sample expectancy sits inside the in-sample bootstrap CI. If it doesn't, I tell you the strategy failed rather than re-fitting.
- A 40-trade paper-forward run lands inside the predicted CI before a dollar is risked.

**Failure signals to watch me for:** a result that only exists at one parameter setting; an equity curve offered in place of a statistic; a Sharpe quoted without the number of configurations tried; any surface observation that doesn't end in a size, a direction, or "stand aside." Any of those, push back.

**What would change my mind about the core verdict:** live ATR materially below my remembered range; a rule set where the trail locks early and generously, which changes the first-passage problem; or an intraday-signal, flat-by-close variant that preserves enough of the daily-bar edge to clear costs — which is the one version of NQ-at-$2K I think might survive, and the first thing I'd test if you rule out MNQ.
