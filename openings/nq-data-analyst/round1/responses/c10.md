## What I think you're actually asking for

You're asking whether a positive-expectancy daily-bar system on NQ can survive a $2,000 trailing equity floor long enough to pass an evaluation. Those are two different problems and the second one dominates. My read is that the brief is arithmetically unsound as written, and I'd want to establish that on day one rather than after three weeks of signal research.

The arithmetic: NQ is $20/point (I'd read this from a cited source, not memory — see day one). $2,000 of drawdown is **100 points of total lifetime room on one contract.** Against an NQ daily ATR I'd put around 400–450 points at current index levels, that's roughly a quarter of one day's range. Even in the calm 2021 regime, with ATR near 200, it's half a day. The conclusion is insensitive to which ATR number is right across the entire plausible range, which is why I'd lead with it.

Put the other way: a daily-timeframe stop is normally 1–1.5× ATR, so 400–600 points, or $8,000–$12,000 on one NQ. That is four to six times the *entire* account allowance. And the minimum position is one contract — there is no sizing lever. If instead you fix risk per trade at a sane fraction of the budget, say 25% ($500), you get a 25-point stop, which is about 6% of a daily range. You'd be stopped out by intraday noise on essentially every trade while calling it a daily strategy.

Three things the brief leaves implicit that I think are more important than the strategy idea:

1. **It doesn't say what kind of drawdown.** Static-from-$50,000 (floor at $48,000, never moves), end-of-day trailing on closed balance, or intraday trailing on peak *unrealized* equity are three different products. Under the last one — common at eval firms — a trade that goes +80 points and comes back to flat has raised your floor by 80 points and you can die having closed the trade at breakeven. Survivability differs by roughly 3–4× between these. This is the single largest unknown in the mandate and no amount of signal research substitutes for reading the rulebook.
2. **It doesn't say whether the firm permits overnight and weekend holds.** Many eval accounts prohibit holding through the 17:00 ET maintenance break or over the weekend, and set overnight margin far above what a $50k simulated account supports. A daily-bar strategy is by definition an overnight-holding strategy. If holds are prohibited, the mandate is dead on contact and nothing else matters.
3. **"$50,000 account size" is nearly a decorative number.** It's simulated; it matters only for margin rules. The real account is $2,000 and the real leverage is 1 NQ. I'd stop referring to it as a $50k account internally because it makes the risk feel four times less tight than it is.

I'd also want to know whether the objective is *passing the evaluation* or *running the funded account afterward*. Passing is a first-passage problem — reach +$3,000 before -$2,000 — and it rewards higher variance. Running funded capital rewards the opposite. Optimizing for one while reporting the other is a good way to be confidently wrong.

The one thing in the mandate's favour, which is worth saying because it inverts the usual finding: **costs are irrelevant here.** NQ round-turn commission at a prop firm is on the order of $4–5 all-in, and with 1–2 ticks of slippage per side on a non-news daily entry you're at maybe $15–25 round turn, about one NQ point against a 400-point range. For most intraday work costs are the thing that kills the edge. Here they're noise. The binding constraint is risk geometry, full stop.

## How I'd approach it

**Phase 0 — Specs, from sources, before anything.** Build `specs.yaml` with point value, tick size and value, contract months, expiry/last-trade rules, CME initial and maintenance margin, exchange and clearing fees, session hours including the maintenance break, and the prop firm's full rule text. Every field carries a source URL and retrieval date; anything older than 90 days gets flagged stale. I don't quote a spec I haven't read this quarter, and the trailing-drawdown mechanics in particular I want as a direct quotation from the rulebook, not a paraphrase.

**Phase 1 — Feasibility arithmetic. This is the phase that probably ends the project.** Stop distance implied by the budget, versus measured ATR(14) across regimes; the distribution of overnight gaps (|session open − prior close|) on session-based bars; the distribution of maximum adverse excursion for a one-day hold. The decisive measurement is **P(overnight adverse move > 100 points)**. If that's above a few percent per night, a 1-lot NQ daily position has a per-night mortality rate, and over the twenty-plus holding nights an evaluation needs, ruin is near-certain regardless of whether the signal is any good. I'd produce this as a one-page memo with the four options — switch to MNQ, shorten the holding period and change the strategy being asked for, raise the drawdown budget, or size it explicitly as a lottery ticket — before I touch a hypothesis.

For contrast, in the same memo: MNQ at $2/point gives 1,000 points of room, roughly 2.4× ATR, and lets you scale 1–10 lots. That's a workable geometry. The mandate as written forbids the only instrument that makes it work.

**Phase 2 — Data and audit, before the hypothesis.** Per-contract OHLCV plus 1-minute bars, ten years, Databento GLBX.MDP3 or the broker's MDP 3.0 feed, Parquet with a checksum manifest. Build the continuous series with my own roll script so the method is inspectable. Audit: bar counts, phantom gaps at the Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec rolls, prices as exact multiples of 0.25, CME holiday and early-close sessions, DST transitions where the US and European offsets diverge for a couple of weeks a year, and session boundaries at Sun 18:00 ET through Fri 17:00 ET with the daily break.

Two roll decisions I'd pre-register rather than default: I'd roll on measured volume crossover rather than assuming a fixed number of days before expiry, and I'd **generate signals on the adjusted series but compute all risk and P&L in points on the unadjusted per-contract price.** Back-adjustment shifts the price level, which silently corrupts any rule referencing round numbers, percentage stops, or absolute levels — and over ten years it can push old prices to nonsense values.

I'd also force a decision on what "daily bar" means: the 23-hour Globex session bar or the RTH-only 09:30–16:00 ET bar. These are materially different series and a large share of NQ's drift arrives overnight. Picking one after seeing which backtests better is a free parameter I'd rather burn up front.

**Phase 3 — Noise characterisation and power, before the strategy.** Realised vol by session and hour (Yang-Zhang and Garman-Klass alongside close-to-close), Ljung-Box on returns, Lo-MacKinlay variance ratios across horizons, a Welch periodogram to distinguish real periodicity from calendar artefacts.

Then the number that governs everything. `SE(SR) ≈ sqrt((1 + SR²/2)/N)`. Ten years of daily bars is about 2,520 observations, so SE on the annualised Sharpe is roughly 0.32. Three years gives about 0.58. So on three years I cannot distinguish an annualised Sharpe of 0.8 from zero — and if I run twenty variants, the best of twenty pure-noise variants will show an annualised Sharpe near **1.2** by construction. I'd state that threshold in writing before looking at any result, and it means: on three years of daily NQ data, a strategy showing Sharpe 1.0 is not evidence of anything. Ten years with twenty trials needs roughly 0.75+ to mean something.

**Phase 4 — Pre-register and seal.** Frozen plan file, git-committed, timestamped: hypothesis, exact rule, exact parameter values chosen from theory or a held-out pre-period, cost model in ticks and dollars, the decision statistic, the threshold. Final 25% of the data sealed and untouched. An append-only `trials.log` that survives across sessions, because trial three next Tuesday is still trial three.

**Phase 5 — Run blind, then quantify.** Event-driven loop in numpy, not a vectorised library backtest — those hide lookahead, and here the path dependence means vectorised P&L is actively misleading. Purged, embargoed walk-forward splits. Then stationary block bootstrap for CIs, Newey-West on any regression, deflated Sharpe against the logged trial count, and probability of backtest overfitting via CSCV.

**Phase 6 — The survival simulation, which is the real test.** Terminal P&L is not the question; whether the equity floor is breached before the profit target is. I'd simulate the equity path bar-by-bar on 1-minute data so intrabar excursions are real, run Monte Carlo over trade orderings, and report the fraction of paths that pass, the fraction that breach, and the fraction that time out — separately under EOD trailing and intraday trailing. A strategy with genuine positive expectancy and a 65% hit rate can easily breach on more than half of paths. That result is the deliverable, not the Sharpe.

**Phase 7 — Stress, then break the seal once.** Costs doubled, fills one tick worse, entry delayed one bar, and the earnings-heavy weeks in late Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct where ATR expands meaningfully. Then the holdout, once. Whatever it says is the answer. If it disagrees with in-sample, the strategy is dead and I report it dead; I don't open a new round of variants on the same data without declaring a trial-count reset and a new holdout.

## Where this goes wrong

**The trailing drawdown is simulated wrong and everything downstream is fiction.** Most backtests report terminal P&L. Under an intraday trail on peak unrealized equity, the floor ratchets on every favourable excursion, including ones on trades that end flat. If I simulate that on daily bars I'll miss the excursions entirely and produce a survival rate that's far too optimistic. *Fix:* bar-by-bar equity path on 1-minute data, both trail variants reported, and the rule text quoted verbatim in the report so you can check I read it right.

**I confirm what everyone already knows about 2023–2025.** NQ went up a great deal in the recent sample and any long-biased daily rule will backtest beautifully. *Fix:* pre-register a vol-scaled buy-and-hold benchmark and require the edge to beat beta rather than zero; run the identical rule on ES and RTY and on NQ 2000–2010 as falsification, not as tuning.

**I overfit the constraint rather than the market.** The most seductive failure here is searching for a rule whose stop happens to fit inside 100 points. That selects on in-sample maximum adverse excursion, which is close to the worst statistic you can select on — it's the one most driven by the specific realised path and least stable out of sample. *Fix:* the stop distance is fixed by ATR multiple and pre-registered before the rule is chosen, never tuned to fit the budget.

**Trial count leaks across days and conversations.** Twenty variants over three weeks feels like one honest attempt if nobody counts. *Fix:* append-only committed `trials.log`, every deflated statistic computed against the cumulative count, and the count printed in the first section of every report.

**Signal/execution collision.** A rule computed from the daily close cannot be filled at that close. *Fix:* entries at next-session open or an explicit stated MOC mechanism, pre-registered, plus a delayed-entry sensitivity run.

**Regime non-stationarity in the risk units.** NQ's ATR roughly doubled from 2021 to now. A fixed point-based stop is a different strategy in each regime. *Fix:* everything in ATR multiples, with results reported by vol tercile.

**Back-adjustment corrupting level-based logic.** Covered above; the fix is separating signal series from P&L series, and I'd show one worked trade both ways in the report as proof.

**I reinvent something finance already named.** Likely, given my background. *Fix:* before implementing any estimator of my own, I spend ten minutes finding its standard name and known failure modes, cite what I found, and if I still write my own I show it agreeing with the standard one on a test case.
