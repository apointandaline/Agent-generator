## What I think you're actually asking for

You've asked for a strategy. What you've actually specified is a **barrier-crossing problem**, and the strategy is downstream of arithmetic that I don't think has been done yet.

The arithmetic, first, because everything else is contingent on it:

$2,000 drawdown ÷ ($20/point × 1 NQ contract) = **100 points of total lifetime room.**

NQ's 14-day ATR has been running roughly 400–450 points in recent regimes — *stale reference, I'd re-pull against current exchange data before quoting it to anyone*. Call it ~420. So the account's entire life is **≈0.24 ATR**. The Globex session alone, 18:00–09:30 ET, routinely ranges 150–250 points. A daily-timeframe position is exposed, every single night, unhedged, to more than the account's total room.

That means the deployment sample size is **one trade**. Not "a small sample" — one. A daily strategy with any defensible stop (≥1 ATR) risks 4× the account on its first entry. At a quarter-ATR stop you get exactly one loser before the floor. **No backtest, however rigorous, informs a decision that will be resolved by a single draw.** That's the thing I'd want on the table before we talk about signals.

Three things the brief leaves dangerously implicit:

1. **The drawdown mechanics are undefined.** $50K/$2,000 smells like Topstep's $50K Combine — if so, there's also a ~$1,000 *daily* loss limit, a 5-contract cap, a $3,000 profit target, and the trail is EOD on closed balance and *locks* once you're +$2,000. Every one of those changes the problem materially. Apex's $50K is $2,500 and trails intraday. I will not guess. A strategy that passes an EOD trail can be killed by an intraday trail on the same trade sequence.
2. **The objective isn't Sharpe.** If the target is $3,000 against $2,000 of room, then with *zero edge* and symmetric bets, P(hit target before floor) = 2000/5000 = **40%**. That's the honest baseline. The job isn't "maximise risk-adjusted return," it's "maximise probability of touching +$3,000 before −$2,000," which is a different optimisation with a different answer.
3. **The micro doesn't rescue this, and I want to kill that idea early.** MNQ gives 1,000 points of room — but the profit target scales down identically, so the barrier ratio stays 1.5:1 and the zero-edge pass probability stays 40%. What micros buy is **granularity**: 5–8 independent bets instead of 1. Granularity converts a real edge into a higher pass probability and does nothing for a fake one. That's the correct argument for MNQ, and it's not the argument people usually make.

**Where I'd push back on the brief:** "daily timeframe, not a scalper" is the constraint most in tension with $2,000. There's a middle register you may not have considered — *daily-bar signal generation with intraday execution and a flat-overnight rule*. That keeps the research on daily structure, which is what you want, and removes the gap exposure the budget cannot absorb. It is not scalping. I'd want to know whether that's inside or outside the mandate.

And the obvious repair fails on its own arithmetic: capping gap risk with an NQ put wing costs a few hundred dollars a night against a $500K notional. At $300–600/night, overnight option protection consumes the entire drawdown budget in under a week. Hedged overnight carry is unaffordable on this account, not merely unattractive.

## How I'd approach it

**Phase 0 — Rules and arithmetic (half a day).** Get the actual rule document. Compute max points of risk, the barrier ratio, the zero-edge ruin probability, and the implied stop against current ATR and against the options-implied move. Gate: if the implied stop sits inside ordinary noise, I stop and bring you four options rather than hunting for an edge inside a budget that can't hold one.

**Phase 1 — Data provenance.** Databento GLBX.MDP3 for NQ and MNQ (`ohlcv-1m`, plus `definitions` for expiries), CME DataMine for official settlements and volume/OI to derive roll dates, and a vendor continuous series only as a reconciliation check. I build the series **three ways** — back-adjusted, ratio-adjusted, and unadjusted per-contract with explicit roll dates — and I reconcile roll dates and settlements across two independent sources before trusting anything multi-year.

Two NQ-specific traps I'd check on day one: **additive back-adjustment drives NQ negative in the early 2000s** once you accumulate 25 years of roll gaps against a contract that fell from ~4,800 to ~800, which silently breaks every percentage-return and z-score feature; and a "daily bar" on a 23-hour product is ambiguous — CME's trading day is 17:00→16:00 ET with a maintenance break, and a vendor cutting bars on UTC midnight splits the session and manufactures gaps that a breakout system will happily trade.

**Phase 2 — Look-ahead hunt, before any performance measurement.** Shift every feature forward one bar and re-run; if performance collapses, the signal was eating information it couldn't have had. The specific killer on daily futures: **settlement is not a tradeable price.** CME equity index settlement is the VWAP of the last 30 seconds of RTH. If the backtest signals on the daily close and fills at the close, it's fictional — fills go at 16:00 marketable or at the next session's open. Same-bar stop-and-target resolution from daily high/low is the other one.

**Phase 3 — Pre-registration.** Split scheme (anchored walk-forward, fixed windows; purged k-fold with embargo ≥ hold + lookback), primary metric, trial budget, pass threshold — all written to the repo with a timestamp *before* I look at out-of-sample results. Trial count logged from the first parameter I try.

**Phase 4 — Costs, honestly.** NQ tick = 0.25 = $5.00; MNQ = $0.50. One tick crossing each way plus a real commission schedule (~$3.10–$4.00 RT all-in on a Tradovate-style prop stack — *verify against the actual firm*) gives ≈2.7 ticks ≈ 0.68 points round turn. Against a 400-point daily range, **cost is not the binding constraint here** — this is the unusual case where I don't get to kill it on cost. Costs enter differently: as **stop-order slippage through an overnight gap**, where the fill is the gap, not the stop. That's what MBP-10 depth is for.

**Phase 5 — Statistics.** Deflated Sharpe with the logged trial count, PSR against zero, PBO via CSCV, Newey–West t-stats, SPA when comparing variants. Plus MinTRL: for a Sharpe of 1.0 to be distinguishable from noise at 95% you need roughly four years of daily data — I'd state that next to any headline number.

**Phase 6 — Path, which is where this is decided.** Monte Carlo over trade-sequence permutations and bootstrap resampling, evaluated against the *actual* rule — EOD trailing for eval, intraday against peak unrealised equity for funded. I report **the percentage of simulated paths that breach the floor**, never the mean equity curve. My prior is that this number exceeds 90% for any daily-hold NQ configuration, and producing it is how I'd demonstrate the kill quantitatively instead of by assertion.

**Phase 7 — Regime.** VXN terciles, by year, day of week, session segment, earnings weeks (late Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct, ATR up 20–35%) — and for NQ specifically, **NVDA's own cycle** (late Feb/May/Aug/Nov), which sits outside the standard earnings weeks and moves the index 1–2% alone. The top handful of names are roughly half the index; NQ is a levered bet on a few earnings events, and any strategy that doesn't survive that partition is a calendar artefact.

**Options, used in the concrete direction:** fit NQ settlement IVs from DataMine with Black-76 (never Black-Scholes on spot), and ask what the surface implies for the distribution over the holding period. If the implied 1-day move is 1.4% and the stop is 0.4%, the market is telling you the stop-out probability before I run a single backtest. The 25-delta risk reversal tells you the overnight gap risk is asymmetric downward, so the ATR-symmetric sizing assumption understates the fatal tail.

## What I'd do in the first day

1. **Get the rule PDF and name the firm.** Twenty minutes, and it determines whether the rest of the work is even the right work.
2. **Build the MAE histogram.** Pull NQ daily 2010–2026, and for every day compute the maximum adverse excursion from a random entry over a 1-day and 3-day hold. Then: what fraction exceed 100 points? I expect 85–95%. That histogram is the day-one deliverable.
3. **Run the ruin number.** Barrier ratio, zero-edge pass probability, and the same under a +0.1 and +0.2 Sharpe assumption, at 1, 3 and 8 bets of granularity. One spreadsheet.
4. **Pull the NQ options-implied 1-day and 3-day move** from the nearest weekly settlement IVs and put it beside the 100-point stop.
5. **Write the pre-registration doc and open the graveyard ledger** with this hypothesis as entry #1, before any signal work exists to bias it.

I'd send you a one-page memo at end of day: histogram, ruin number, implied move, and a go/no-go on the mandate as written. No signal research until you've seen it.

## Where this goes wrong

**The project spends three weeks finding an edge inside an infeasible budget.** The most likely failure isn't a bad backtest — it's a good one, on a mandate that can't hold it. *Mitigation:* the Phase 0 gate is a hard stop with a written verdict, not a checkpoint I can talk past.

**Roll artefact.** A daily NQ strategy showing an edge on back-adjusted data and nothing on per-contract data is trading the roll gaps, not the market. *Mitigation:* every result reported on all three series; disagreement is an automatic kill.

**Settlement-as-fill look-ahead.** Inflates a daily strategy by more than most people expect. *Mitigation:* one-bar shift test, and fills resolved only at the next tradeable print.

**Intrabar optimism.** Vectorised engines resolve stop-and-target ordering in whichever direction flatters you. *Mitigation:* I write the event-driven engine myself for anything with stops; vectorbt only for triage, and I say when a number came from triage.

**Multiple testing.** Fifteen entry rules × six stop multiples × four filters = 360 trials, and a raw Sharpe of 1.4 is what 360 draws from noise looks like. *Mitigation:* trial count logged from trial one; DSR reported with it; a second pass after a failure restarts the count and the report says so.

**Passing the eval and dying funded.** The trail changes from EOD-closed to intraday-unrealised. A strategy with wide MFE-then-giveback trades passes one and fails the other. *Mitigation:* both rule sets simulated on every candidate; a conflict escalates to you rather than getting resolved by me.

**The seductive dead end.** High win rate, wide stop, "it always comes back." On a trailing drawdown there is no recovery path once the floor ratchets — one adverse sequence is terminal regardless of long-run expectancy. I won't model martingale or averaging down here, and I'd flag it if it shows up in someone else's proposal.

**My own failure mode.** I'm faster at destruction than construction, and the natural output of this process is a graveyard. So: every kill I hand you ships with at least one falsifiable repair — the change to product, holding period, data or execution that would flip the verdict — or an explicit statement that I can't think of one, which is a stronger claim I'd have to defend. And I timebox: if I've spent the agreed budget without either killing it or clearing it, I hand you the ambiguity rather than keep digging.
