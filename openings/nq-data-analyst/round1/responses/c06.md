## What I think you're actually asking for

You're asking me to build a strategy. What you've actually handed me is a **first-passage problem with an absorbing boundary**, and the strategy is the small part.

Here is the arithmetic that has to come before anything else. NQ is $20/point, $5/tick. A $2,000 maximum drawdown on one contract is:

```
$2,000 / $20 per point = 100 points of total account life
```

Not 100 points per trade. 100 points of cumulative adverse excursion from the equity high-water mark, ever, across the whole evaluation. NQ's 14-day RTH ATR has recently run in the ~410–450 point area. So the entire account is worth roughly **0.24 of one average session's range.**

Three things the brief leaves dangerously implicit:

1. **"Maximum drawdown" is almost certainly a *trailing* drawdown, not a static one.** If this is a Topstep-style $50k combine, the floor starts at $48,000, follows your equity high up, and only locks (at $50,000) once you reach $52,000. Until you've banked the first $2,000 of profit, the account never gets safer — every dollar you make tightens the noose by a dollar. That's a gauntlet, and it's the dominant term in the pass probability. It needs modelling explicitly and separately from the post-lock phase.
2. **Whether the trail measures intraday equity (unrealised, tick-by-tick) or end-of-day closing balance.** These are two completely different strategies and I would report both. A daily-bar backtest cannot evaluate an intraday trail at all — a trade that closes +40 after being −180 at 11:15 looks like a winner on daily bars and is a blown account under tick-by-tick trailing. If it's intraday-trailing, I need 1-minute or tick data even though we're building a daily strategy. That's not optional.
3. **"Daily timeframe" and "$2,000 drawdown" are, as far as I can currently tell, mutually exclusive.** A daily-horizon strategy has a structural stop set by market structure — prior swing, session extreme, N×ATR — which on NQ today is several hundred points. Let me define *N* = number of full stop-losses the budget absorbs before ruin. You want N ≥ 8–10, because losses cluster and trade outcomes are autocorrelated within a regime.

| Sizing | $/pt | Stop that gives N=10 | That stop in ATR units (ATR≈425) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 NQ | $20 | 10 pts | 0.02× |
| 1 MNQ | $2 | 100 pts | 0.24× |
| 1 MNQ, aggressive N=4 | $2 | 250 pts | 0.59× |

There is **no contract-and-size combination under a $2,000 trailing drawdown that supports a genuine daily-horizon structural stop with a survivable number of losses.** MNQ doesn't fix this and I want to be precise about why: MNQ doesn't create room, it creates *granularity*. The room comes from accepting one-tenth the exposure. At 1 MNQ with a 1-ATR stop you're still risking $850, or 42.5% of the entire account, on one trade.

So my honest read: you're asking me to find out whether the mandate is possible, and you'd rather I tell you in week one that it isn't than in month three that it is. The deliverable I'd expect to be judged on is a defensible **probability of passing**, not an equity curve.

## How I'd approach it

**Phase 0 — Restate the mandate as constraints (hours, not days).**
Read the actual rule document, not a summary. Enumerate: trail type (EOD vs intraday), lock level and trigger, whether unrealised PnL counts, daily loss limit, profit target, minimum trading days, consistency/best-day rule, scaling plan, flat-by-time rule, holiday and roll-week handling, eval fee and reset cost. Every item I can't read in the document becomes a numbered question to you, not an assumption. Then the range-to-budget table above, with the real numbers.

**Phase 1 — Characterise the instrument before the strategy.**
I don't have a view on NQ yet and I'm not going to pretend one. What I want empirically, from NQ itself:
- Distribution of RTH range and of full Globex range (they differ, and "daily" needs defining — the CME daily bar is the 18:00–17:00 ET Globex session, not the 09:30–16:00 one).
- Overnight gap distribution, prior settle → 09:30 open. A daily strategy holds overnight. If the median absolute gap is anywhere near 100 points, the gap alone ends the NQ version of this mandate and nothing else needs computing.
- **Maximum adverse excursion distribution** for a swing-style entry: from entry, how far against you before the trade resolves. This is the statistic that meets the trailing floor, not the stop distance.
- ATR by quarter and by year, and the NQ/ES range ratio (typically 1.3–1.5×).
- Volatility clustering (EGARCH), so simulated paths aren't i.i.d. when the real ones aren't.
- VXN term structure and the variance risk premium (implied minus realised at matched horizon) as a regime tag — that's the signal that most reliably tells you a futures strategy is about to meet a different volatility world than the one it was fitted in.

**Critically, everything gets expressed in ATR units, not points.** NQ's ATR was 60–120 points in 2015 and is 400+ now. A fixed $2,000 point-budget has lost ~5× of its room over that history. Any backtest that uses points-based stops across 2015–2026 is comparing incommensurable things, and will look far safer than it is.

**Phase 2 — Data spine, declared not implied.**
Databento `GLBX.MDP3`, NQ, 1-minute OHLCV for path-dependent work plus per-contract daily. Roll on volume crossover (in practice the Thursday before the second Friday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec, but I'd verify against volume, not calendar folklore). I'd hold **two** series: ratio-adjusted for return/signal research, and unadjusted per-contract for fill and PnL simulation. Back-adjustment matters here more than usual — quarterly carry on NQ at ~4.5% rates and ~24,000 index minus ~0.7% NDX yield is roughly 200+ points per quarter, so a back-adjusted series drifts on the order of 800 points/year against actually tradable prices. Against a 100-point budget that's not a rounding error, it's phantom PnL. Session filter and the 17:00–18:00 maintenance break stated explicitly.

**Phase 3 — Specify rules as executable code with costs.**
Commission ~$4 RT on NQ, ~$1.20 on MNQ; slippage assumption stated and defended (I'd start at 1 tick per side on limits, 2 on stops, and check it against tick data at the times of day we'd actually trade). No fills at bar extremes. Entry and exit on the 0.25 grid.

**Phase 4 — In-sample fit under a declared trial budget.**
I write down the number of configurations before testing — say 24 — and I don't exceed it without saying so in the report. Every headline number gets deflated for that count.

**Phase 5 — Fit the loss tail, not the loss average.**
A daily strategy over five years might give 300–500 trades but only ~3–4 independent volatility regimes. If losing trades number under ~30, I credibility-weight the fitted tail (Bühlmann–Straub) against a prior built from NQ's own range distribution, rather than quoting the empirical worst loss as if it were the worst possible loss. Peaks-over-threshold GPD with a mean-residual-life plot for threshold selection; expected shortfall at 97.5% per trade and per day.

**Phase 6 — Simulate survival, not performance.**
Stationary block bootstrap over the trade sequence, block length chosen to preserve observed autocorrelation, 50,000+ paths, applying the *actual* trailing rule — intraday MAE against a moving floor if that's the rule. Outputs: P(ruin before target) with bootstrap CI, P(reaching the lock at +$2,000 before the floor) as a separate number, median and 10th-percentile trades-to-failure, Kaplan–Meier survival curve with band, and the MAE distribution plotted directly against the live floor.

**Phase 7 — Try to kill it.**
Purged walk-forward with embargo. PBO via combinatorially symmetric cross-validation. Hansen SPA against the whole trial family, not just the winner. Re-run under a 1.5× ATR regime and under earnings-week inflation (late Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct, ranges typically +20–35%). Anything that survives only in its fitting regime is reported as failed.

The order matters: Phase 0 can kill the project in an afternoon for the cost of an afternoon, and Phases 5–7 cost weeks. I'm not spending the weeks until the afternoon says it's worth it.

## Where this goes wrong

**Evaluating an intraday trailing rule on daily bars.** The single most likely way this project produces a confidently wrong answer. A daily-bar backtest sees closes; the trail sees every tick. Mitigation: all survival simulation runs on 1-minute path data with per-trade MAE, and I report EOD-trailing and intraday-trailing as two separate strategies with two separate ruin probabilities.

**Back-adjustment phantom PnL.** A continuous series with additive back-adjustment produces prices that never traded and gaps that never happened. Mitigation: dual series, PnL computed on unadjusted per-contract prices, adjustment method named in the report.

**Points-vs-ATR non-stationarity.** Backtesting a 100-point budget over 2015–2019 makes it look like three ATRs of room. Mitigation: all thresholds expressed in ATR units and converted to points at the simulated regime's volatility, with the vol regime drawn from a fitted EGARCH rather than held fixed.

**Undeclared trials.** Testing four entry filters × three stop multiples × two session windows and reporting the best one as if it were the only one. Mitigation: trial count declared before testing, in the report, with a deflated Sharpe computed against it, plus PBO. I won't publish a backtest without a trial count, and that includes trials spent on choosing the instrument and timeframe — if I test ES and NQ and daily and 4-hour, that multiplies the count and I say so.

**Thin loss sample.** A daily strategy may yield 40 losing trades across three regimes. The empirical worst loss is then a weak estimate of the tail and a terrible estimate of the bound. Mitigation: credibility-weighted GPD tail, effective sample size after autocorrelation correction reported next to raw trade count, and the words "extrapolated" next to any tail quantile beyond the observed data.

**Optimising the wrong objective.** If the real goal is passing an evaluation that costs a monthly fee and can be reset, the correct objective may be P(pass) net of fees over a sequence of attempts — which has a very different optimum from maximising expectancy, and may favour higher variance. That's your decision, not mine, but I'd name it rather than silently optimise Sharpe.

**Rule interactions nobody modelled.** Consistency rules (no single day >50% of total profit), minimum trading days, flat-by-time requirements, and scaling plans can fail an account that never touched the drawdown floor. A low-frequency daily strategy is especially exposed — it may simply not generate enough trading days inside the window. Mitigation: these go into the simulation as hard constraints, not footnotes.

**Reporting evaluation results as funded results.** Funded-account rules usually differ (different trail, different lock, payout rules, drawdown mechanics). I won't present one as the other.

**The thing I'm most likely to be wrong about: microstructure.** I have no futures trading experience. I learned this complex from specifications and data. My slippage assumptions, my sense of liquidity in the first ten minutes, and my sense of roll-week behaviour are untrusted until checked against tick data or against someone who has traded it. If someone with floor experience tells me a fill assumption is wrong, my prior is that they're right and I go get the tick data to settle it rather than argue from the model.

And the one I won't negotiate: I will not design or validate a martingale, grid, or averaging-down structure under a trailing floor. The absorbing boundary makes them unsurvivable regardless of expectancy, and no parameter choice changes that.

## How you'll know it worked

**Within the first week**, success is that you can state, from memory, how many full stop-losses this account absorbs before it's dead, and that the four options are on one page in front of you. If I've spent a week and you still have to ask me whether the mandate is feasible, I've failed regardless of what I built.

**At the end of the project**, success looks like a number with an interval on it: "P(pass) = 0.31, 95% CI [0.24, 0.38], across 50,000 bootstrapped paths under intraday trailing, 24 configurations evaluated, deflated Sharpe 0.4." Not an equity curve. If I hand you an equity curve as the headline result, that's a failure signal and you should treat it as one.

**A defensible "no" is a success.** If the answer is "this mandate cannot be met on NQ, here is the MNQ version with a 30% pass probability and here is what a $6,000 drawdown budget would buy you," that's a win — it's saved the eval fees and the months.

**Concrete falsification thresholds, written down before any live trading:**
- Live per-trade MAE distribution must be inside the simulated distribution — a KS test against the simulated MAE at 20 trades, and specifically the worst observed MAE should sit below the simulated 90th percentile.
- Realised slippage in ticks within the assumed range, measured, not estimated.
- Realised ATR within the regime band the strategy was validated across; if NQ ATR moves outside that band, the strategy is out of scope until re-validated, not "under pressure."
- If any of those breach, we stop. Written down now, not argued about later.

**Failure signals you should watch me for:** a backtest without a trial count; a maximum drawdown quoted from a single path as though it were a bound; a result presented under EOD trailing when the account trails intraday; a wide stop justified by a high win rate; or me spending more than a day modelling something the ATR comparison already answered. Any of those and you should push back hard.
