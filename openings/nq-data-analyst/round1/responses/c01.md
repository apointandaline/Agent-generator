## What I think you're actually asking for

The brief asks for a daily NQ strategy. Before any of that, here is the number that decides the project:

**`max_points_of_risk` = $2,000 ÷ ($20/pt × 1 contract) = 100 NQ points.** That is the entire account, from open to death, for one contract. NQ New York-session 14-day ATR has recently been running ~410–450 points — a regime estimate with a shelf life that I would re-measure on day one, not assume. **Budget ÷ ATR ≈ 0.23×.**

The whole account is less than a quarter of one average day's range. On a *daily* timeframe — where a stop has to sit outside daily noise, so 0.5–1.0× ATR, i.e. 200–450 points, i.e. $4,000–9,000 per contract — the first stop is two to four and a half times the total drawdown budget. The account cannot survive a single correctly-placed daily stop. That is not a signal problem and no amount of research fixes it.

Three things the brief leaves dangerously implicit:

1. **"Maximum drawdown" is not a rule set.** Trailing or static? Measured end-of-day on closed equity, or intraday against peak *unrealised* equity? Does the trail lock at initial balance + profit, or keep ratcheting? Daily loss limit? Minimum trading days? Consistency rule (most $50k evals cap any single day at 30–50% of total profit)? Profit target (usually $3,000)? Scaling plan capping contracts during evaluation? And where do funded rules diverge from evaluation rules? Under an **intraday** trail, a daily hold that goes +80 points and gives it back kills the account with zero losing trades. Under **EOD**, it doesn't. Same strategy, opposite verdict. I will not model until I have the document or written assumptions I can quote back.
2. **Most evaluation accounts prohibit or penalise overnight and weekend holds**, or force flat before the close. "Daily timeframe" may be contractually impossible on this account before it is arithmetically impossible. Separately, NQ overnight initial margin is roughly $25–30k (re-check the current CME bulletin) against a $50k account — you can hold one, and margin is still not the binding constraint; the $2,000 is.
3. **Gap risk makes the stop notional.** Held overnight, your 100-point budget is exposed to a single print. Late-Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct earnings weeks expand NQ ATR 20–35%, and one NVDA or MSFT night has moved NQ 1.5–2.5% — 400–700 points. A stop does not protect you through that.

So my read: you want a rule-based, low-attention, overnight-capable NQ strategy that passes a $50k evaluation. Two of those four are mutually exclusive as specified.

## How I'd approach it

**Phase 0 — rule extraction (hours, not days).** Read the firm's rule document and fee schedule. Extract the ten fields above. Flag every evaluation-vs-funded divergence. Any ambiguity that moves the modelled result by more than ~10% on pass probability comes back to you as a question, not an assumption I quietly make.

**Phase 1 — arithmetic screen, before a single price bar loads.** `max_points_of_risk` for NQ, MNQ, ES, MES against current measured ATR on the intended holding period. This is the phase that, on the numbers above, already returns *untradeable as written*. It is capped at the front of the project; I don't re-litigate it mid-analysis.

**Phase 2 — reconstruction, costed.** A rejection without a viable alternative is a veto machine, not an analyst. I cost at least two:

- **NQ → MNQ.** $2/pt turns 100 points of room into **1,000** — budget ÷ ATR = **2.33×**. A 0.5-ATR stop (≈215 pts) costs $430 = **21.5% of budget**, giving ~4.6 maximum-risk losses before the floor. That is thin but it is a geometry that exists. Honest arithmetic on the rest: a $3,000 target on 1 MNQ is 1,500 net points; at 215-point risk, 1.5:1 payoff and a 50% win rate that's ~28 trades, which at daily frequency is 3–6 months — and the probability of a 4-loss net drawdown somewhere in 28 trades is on the order of 50%+ before the trailing ratchet makes it worse. I expect the block bootstrap to confirm that, and if it does, MNQ-on-daily is *also* a fail — just a narrower one.
- **Shorten the holding period.** If risk is taken in a defined intraday window, the relevant volatility measure becomes session range (~150–200 points on the NY open two hours), not daily ATR. A 40-point stop on MNQ is $80 = **4% of budget** — 25 losses of room. This is a viable account. It is also **not the strategy you asked for**, and I'd rather say that in one line than smuggle it in.
- Also on the table, stated plainly: raise the drawdown budget (1 NQ on a daily hold with a 1-ATR stop needs ~$25–30k of drawdown for a three-loss tolerance — that's a different account product), or run it as a declared lottery ticket where the eval fee is the option premium and I give you P(pass) so you can price it.

**Phase 3 — data.** Databento GLBX.MDP3 per-contract, MBP-1 where fill realism matters; CME DataMine as cross-check. Never a vendor continuous series for final numbers. Roll on volume/open-interest crossover (NQ volume migrates roughly a week before the third-Friday expiry). Build both back-adjusted and unadjusted series and confirm they produce identical trade-level points before trusting either. Declare the session explicitly: Globex Sun 18:00 → Fri 17:00 ET with the 17:00–18:00 maintenance break, and state whether a "daily" bar is the RTH 09:30–16:00 bar or the 18:00–17:00 Globex bar — they are different series and produce different signals.

**Phase 4 — specify rules executable, then backtest once.** Entry, exit, stop, target, max contracts, time filter, behaviour across the roll and around FOMC/CPI/NFP and earnings weeks. Costs in: commission per round turn from *their* schedule (call it ~$4 NQ, ~$1.20 MNQ pending confirmation) and slippage in ticks defended by the product — 1 tick mid-session MNQ, 2–4 ticks on NQ stop-markets at the open or on a release, and gap fills modelled at the next actual print rather than at the stop price. Record per-trade MAE and MFE, not just PnL.

**Phase 5 — out of sample.** Walk-forward with purged, embargoed splits; combinatorial purged CV if trade count supports it. IS vs OOS expectancy side by side with the degradation named.

**Phase 6 — survival, the actual decision gate.** Hand-written event-driven loop, tick-ordered, replaying the intraday path of every holding period, because a vectorised daily-bar backtest cannot price an intraday trailing floor and will silently understate failure. OHLC intrabar ambiguity resolved with ticks, not assumptions. Monte Carlo over shuffled orderings *and* stationary block bootstrap — blocks preserve the losing streaks that kill trailing accounts. Outputs: **P(floor before target)**, time-to-pass distribution, drawdown at the 5th/50th/95th percentile path, longest losing streak at the 95th percentile.

**Phase 7 — is the edge distinguishable from the search?** Deflated Sharpe against the declared trial count, Newey–West-adjusted t-stat on per-trade returns, SPA/reality-check if I scanned a family. There is a structural problem here I'd flag now: a daily-frequency strategy over 2015–2026 generates maybe 150–400 trades *total*, and you cannot manufacture more without walking into a different volatility regime. I would compute the PSR minimum track record length required to separate the observed Sharpe from zero. My prior is that it exceeds the trade count the strategy will ever produce — which is a second, independent kill, on statistics rather than arithmetic.

Search budget declared before the first run, every run logged, run count reported. An unreported search is an undisclosed overfit.

## What I'd do in the first day

1. Request the rule document, the commission/fee schedule, and a yes/no on overnight and weekend holds. That last one may end the project by lunch.
2. Pull NQ front-month from Databento, roll on volume crossover, and measure — not assume — current 14-day NY-session ATR, the frequency of sessions whose range exceeds 100 points, and the distribution of the 16:00 → next-09:30 overnight gap.
3. Compute the number that matters most: take entry at each daily close over the last three years and measure maximum adverse excursion over the next session. **The fraction of nights where that MAE ≥ 100 points is, to a first approximation, the per-trade probability of account death at 1 NQ.** I expect it to be well north of half. If it is, the mandate is over and no signal research is warranted.
4. Repeat steps 2–3 for MNQ at 1,000 points of room, and for a 09:30–11:30 intraday window, so the rejection ships with two costed reconstructions rather than a shrug.
5. Write the one-page screen memo: verdict line, the arithmetic, the two reconstructions, what I still need.

That is day one. No indicators, no signal hunting.

## Where this goes wrong

- **Vectorised daily-bar backtest under an intraday trailing floor.** It will report a passing strategy that dies in week one. *Mitigation:* tick-ordered event loop; every trailing-floor evaluation runs against the intraday path, never the bar.
- **Wide stop justified by a high win rate.** Under a trailing floor the account has no tolerance for the one loss, whatever the hundred-trade expectancy says. *Mitigation:* stop distance as a fraction of budget is a hard constraint before the win rate is allowed into the conversation.
- **Noise-tight stops that "pass" the arithmetic.** If P(pass) only clears 50% at a stop under ~0.3× ATR, the strategy is being stopped by microstructure and the backtest edge is a fill artefact. *Mitigation:* report stop ÷ session ATR on every result; below 0.3× triggers explicit MBP-1 fill modelling.
- **Sample-size starvation dressed as an edge.** 180 daily trades gives a 95% CI on expectancy several points wide in both directions. *Mitigation:* report the CI next to the point estimate every time, and the PSR minimum track record length.
- **Optimisation creep.** *Mitigation:* declared budget, logged runs, deflated Sharpe; if it only works after the budget is spent I report it as not working and tell you how many runs it took.
- **Back-adjustment artefacts.** A difference-adjusted series can create signals at roll boundaries that never existed. *Mitigation:* reconcile adjusted and unadjusted trade-level points before any result is trusted.
- **Event contamination.** A backtest whose edge is concentrated in three FOMC days or one earnings week. *Mitigation:* expectancy reported with event days excluded as a separate column.
- **Evaluation-vs-funded divergence.** A configuration that passes the eval and dies funded. *Mitigation:* both rule sets modelled separately, both results shown.
- **Regime drift.** Every ATR figure here has a shelf life. *Mitigation:* if ATR moves materially after delivery, I come back and tell you the prior result is void.

## How you'll know it worked

**Success is not an equity curve.** Concretely, I ship something you can act on when:

- **P(hit the floor before the target)**, from tick-ordered simulation of your exact rule set under block bootstrap, is below a threshold you set in advance — I'd argue for 25% — with the 95th-percentile losing streak and the 5th-percentile equity path shown beside it.
- **OOS expectancy is within a stated degradation of IS**, with the degradation named rather than buried; I'd treat >50% decay as a fail.
- **Deflated Sharpe is positive at the actual trial count I disclose**, and the trade count clears the PSR minimum track record length. If it doesn't, I tell you the edge is indistinguishable from the search, whatever the curve looks like.
- **Stop distance ÷ session ATR sits in a defensible band**, and expectancy survives the slippage assumption being doubled.
- **Forward validation:** one MNQ on a live simulated evaluation for 4–6 weeks, comparing realised slippage and fill quality against the modelled assumptions. If realised costs exceed the model, the backtest is void and I say so.

**Failure signals I would report as loudly as success:** the arithmetic screen comes back at 0.23× and you decide to trade it anyway; the strategy only passes after the search budget is exhausted; pass probability is sensitive to whether the trail is EOD or intraday, which means we are modelling a rule we don't actually have.

And the most likely honest outcome, which I'd rather say now than in three weeks: **as written this is untradeable, and the binding number is 100 points of lifetime risk against ~430 points of daily ATR.** What I'd bring back instead is a costed MNQ reconstruction and a costed holding-period reconstruction, worked to the same depth as the rejection, so you have something to trade rather than a well-argued no.
