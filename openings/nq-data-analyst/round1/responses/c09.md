## What I think you're actually asking for

The brief is four numbers, and three of them are in conflict. Before any strategy talk:

**[arithmetic] The room is 100 points.** $2,000 ÷ ($20/point × 1 contract) = 100 NQ points, total, for the life of the account. NQ's 14-day New York-session ATR is, from memory and pending re-measurement in this run, somewhere around 400–450 points. The entire account's drawdown budget is roughly a quarter of one average day's range. A single overnight session routinely travels more than 100 points with nothing happening. That is not a tight stop; that is an account that can be ended by an ordinary Tuesday.

**[arithmetic] "Daily timeframe" and "prop evaluation account" may be structurally incompatible.** A daily strategy holds overnight. Most evaluation accounts either force flat before the session close, prohibit weekend holds, or require exchange initial margin to carry a position — and NQ initial margin is on the order of $25k/contract (recalled, must be verified against the current CME performance bond table), which a $50k simulated account with a $2k loss limit cannot meaningfully post. If the firm's rules forbid overnight holds, you have not asked for a daily strategy. You have asked for a strategy that must open and close inside one RTH session, which is a different hypothesis space with different economics.

**[arithmetic] The binding constraint is probably not the one you named.** On $50k evaluation accounts there is usually also a daily loss limit (often ~$1,000), a contract cap, and a consistency rule capping the share of total profit from your best day. The consistency rule is the quiet killer: a positive-skew daily strategy that makes its money in three days out of sixty will satisfy the drawdown rule and fail the payout rule.

**[arithmetic] Whether the trail is intraday or end-of-day inverts the strategy design.** If the trailing floor ratchets on *unrealized* equity highs, then maximum favorable excursion is a liability, not an asset: a trade that runs +150 and closes +40 has permanently moved your floor up 150 and handed back 110 points of your 100-point budget. Under that rule, wide-stop trend-following — the natural daily-timeframe NQ approach — is mechanically self-destructive even when profitable. Under an EOD trail, it is fine. Same strategy, opposite verdict, and the difference is one sentence in a PDF I have not read.

**And the objective function in the brief is wrong.** This is not a Sharpe-maximisation problem. It is a first-passage problem: maximise P(reach the profit target before touching the trailing floor), where the floor moves. Those have different optima. I would rather be measured on Δ P(pass) than on backtested Sharpe, and I would want the reset fee treated openly as the premium on an option — if the pass probability is 20% and a reset is ~$165, the expected cost of reaching a funded account is ~$800, and that is the actual economics of the mandate.

## How I'd approach it

**Phase 0 — Restate the mandate as arithmetic (half a day).** Points of room vs measured ATR; measured, not recalled. Extract from the firm's rule document: trail type (intraday vs EOD), what the trail measures (balance vs equity), where it locks, daily loss limit, contract cap, flat-by time, overnight/weekend policy, consistency rule, profit target, minimum trading days, reset cost. If points of room < ~0.5 × ATR, I come back to you before writing a line of strategy code with four options: (a) MNQ at $2/point, 10× finer granularity — 2 MNQ gives 500 points of room instead of 100; (b) shorten the holding period and accept it is a different strategy from the one you asked for; (c) raise the drawdown budget; (d) proceed as an explicitly priced lottery ticket with a stated pass probability. That is your trade-off to make, not mine.

**Phase 1 — Fix contract mechanics in writing.** NQ: $20/point, 0.25 tick = $5.00/tick; MNQ $2/point, $0.50/tick. Globex Sunday 18:00 ET to Friday 17:00 ET with a 17:00–18:00 ET daily maintenance halt; RTH 09:30–16:00 ET; daily settlement from the 14:59:30–15:00:00 CT window; quarterly H/M/U/Z cycle, cash-settled to the SOQ on the third Friday. As of today the front month is NQZ6 — the September roll happened around the 10th and NQU6 settles this morning. I write this down because a "daily bar" is ambiguous: the exchange day runs 18:00→17:00 ET, which is not the RTH day, and choosing one over the other changes every gap statistic you will later quote at me.

**Phase 2 — Build the data before the idea.** Databento GLBX.MDP3, 1-minute OHLCV plus the definitions file for roll dates, back to at least 2015 so the sample contains multiple volatility regimes (2018Q4, 2020, 2022, 2024 August, 2025 spring — recalled list, to be confirmed by measuring realised vol by quarter). Roll on volume crossover. Two series, never one: ratio-adjusted for signal generation and for anything involving returns or ATR-as-percentage, and unadjusted per-contract for PnL accounting. Panama back-adjustment on 25 years of NQ can drive the adjusted series toward or below zero and corrupt any percentage-based calculation; that is exactly the kind of phantom PnL I have shipped before and will not ship again. Plot every roll seam and confirm I did not invent returns.

**Phase 3 — Characterise the instrument and build the null model.** Before any hypothesis: volatility by hour and session; the overnight-versus-RTH return split; the maintenance-break gap distribution against the 100-point budget; earnings-week ATR expansion (late Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct, historically +20–35%, recalled); NQ/ES range ratio (1.3–1.5×, recalled); and the MFE-giveback distribution, because that is what prices the intraday-trail ratchet. Then one specific measurement: from the RTH open, the empirical probability of touching −40 points before +80. If a 40-point stop on NQ is touched by noise 60% of the time, the strategy is not a directional strategy, it is a noise-timing strategy, and I want that on the table early.

**Phase 4 — The zero-edge baseline.** Simulate a coin-flipper trading the exact account rules on a random walk calibrated to measured NQ vol, and report their pass rate. Every subsequent result is expressed as uplift over that number. This is the single most useful thing I can give you in week one, and it costs a day.

**Phase 5 — Hypothesis, then split, then test.** One sentence with an economic mechanism and the conditions under which the mechanism stops holding. Declare in-sample, out-of-sample and an untouched hold-out *in a committed file* before running anything. Costs attached from the first run — never a frictionless pass first, because the frictionless number anchors judgement. Cost model in ticks and dollars: NQ round-turn commission and fees on the order of $2–4.50 all-in (recalled; I will pull the firm's actual schedule), entries at the touch or crossing, and **stops modelled at 2–4 ticks adverse, not 1**. For one lot, size is not the problem — NQ's inside book is thin (single-digit contracts, versus ES's much deeper top-of-book) but one contract clears it. The problem is stop orders becoming market orders through an 08:30 ET print.

**Phase 6 — Deflate, walk forward, hold out once.** Every configuration evaluated is logged. Deflated Sharpe and PBO via CSCV reported beside every raw Sharpe; a single sharp peak in the parameter surface is treated as noise, a plateau is treated as signal. Walk-forward, then the hold-out, one run, recorded whatever it says.

**Phase 7 — Monte Carlo the path, not the mean.** Resample trade orderings and block-bootstrap returns to get the distribution of maximum drawdown and P(hit the floor) under both trailing variants. Expectancy over 100 trades is irrelevant if the account dies on trade 11.

**Phase 8 — Verdict:** TRADE / TRADE SMALL / NOT PROVEN / DEAD, with a numeric kill condition attached.

## What I'd do in the first day

1. Read the prop firm's rule document and extract the ten numbers above into a one-page mechanics file. If the trail's basis or lock point is ambiguous in the text, I email the firm and ask rather than assume — the assumption is worth more than the strategy.
2. Write the arithmetic memo: 100 points of room, measured current NQ ATR beside it, implied stop, and the four options. Send it to you the same day. Nothing downstream matters if you look at that ratio and change the mandate.
3. Pull NQ 1-minute data from Databento with the definitions file, build both the ratio-adjusted and unadjusted series, and plot the roll seams. Timestamps checked against ET with DST handled explicitly, and the 17:00–18:00 break confirmed present in the data rather than silently filled.
4. Measure four distributions: overnight range, RTH range, P(−40 before +80 from the open), and MFE giveback on a naive daily breakout. These tell us whether the mandate is survivable before we know whether any edge exists.
5. Run the zero-edge first-passage simulation under both trail variants and report the coin-flipper's pass rate.

By end of day one you should have a yes/no on whether we are building a strategy or repricing the mandate. You should not have a strategy idea, and you should be suspicious of me if I hand you one.

## Where this goes wrong

**Phantom PnL from the roll.** A back-adjusted NQ series used for both signals and accounting manufactures gaps and returns that never happened. *Mitigation:* two series, seams plotted, PnL reconciled trade-by-trade against unadjusted per-contract prices for a sample month.

**The wrong objective.** Optimising Sharpe on a book with an absorbing barrier produces a strategy that is excellent on average and dies. *Mitigation:* headline metric is P(floor) and Δ P(pass) over the zero-edge baseline; Sharpe is reported but never alone.

**Optimising for the evaluation, then trading the funded account.** These are different rule sets — different trail, different lock, different consistency requirements. A strategy tuned to pass will not be the strategy to trade after passing. *Mitigation:* both rule sets specified up front, and every result reported under both. If a strategy only works under one, that is stated in the verdict.

**Selection artifacts.** With a 100-point budget the parameter search will find something. It always does. *Mitigation:* trial count logged and reported beside every Sharpe, deflated Sharpe and PBO, hold-out run exactly once, and I will not move the split after seeing results.

**Concentration in a handful of days.** If the edge lives in five sessions across ten years, it is a CPI-print lottery, and it will also fail the consistency rule. *Mitigation:* re-run with the worst and best 1% of days removed, and separately with only those days; report how much PnL lives in conditions we could not reliably trade.

**Fill fantasy.** Stops through an 08:30 print or the 09:30 open do not fill at the stop. *Mitigation:* slippage at 1, 2 and 4 ticks; the open and first five minutes excluded in a variant run; fill assumption stated per entry and exit.

**Session and timestamp errors.** DST, the 18:00 day boundary, settlement versus last trade. *Mitigation:* explicit checks in the data-build script, and a known-good day reconciled by hand.

**[judgement] Me.** I closed a fund on a drawdown, and I spent a year writing why. I am prone to seeing that drawdown in setups that do not resemble it and to demanding a burden of proof no research programme can meet — which produces a desk that trades nothing. That failure is quieter than a blown account, not cheaper. *Mitigation:* every objection I raise is tagged [arithmetic] or [judgement]; a [judgement] objection comes with the specific measurement that would settle it, or I withdraw it. I track my veto rate and report it. If I have killed everything, that is evidence about me.
